# Orylkhaevex

> The ship carried no passengers. Every cabin remembered a departure.

## A Vessel beyond the Harbor

Orylkhaevex is the name painted upon a vessel that appears beyond the harbor during nights without a tide. Its sails are dark, its deck is pale, and its anchor hangs above the water instead of beneath it.

Harbor witnesses disagree about its size. Some describe a fishing boat. Others describe a ship large enough to carry the town. All report the same window near the stern, illuminated by a lamp whose flame leans toward the open sea.

No boat reaches the vessel. Those who row toward it find themselves returning with salt upon their backs and a small brass key beneath their seats. The keys fit no lock in the harbor. They grow colder whenever the ship's window becomes dark.

## The Captain's Empty Coat

A coat washed ashore during a storm that never touched the town. Its sleeves were wet, but its collar held dry sand. Inside one pocket, a folded chart showed the harbor as seen from beneath the seabed.

The harbor master hung the coat in his office. By morning, every button reflected a different cabin. Some cabins contained beds. Some contained closed doors. One contained an enormous pale eye, looking toward a window whose lamp was visible somewhere behind it.

He covered that button with cloth. The coat became too heavy for its hook. When he removed the cloth, the weight vanished, and a brass key fell from the empty sleeve.

The key bore a number that changed whenever anyone counted the ship's windows. The harbor master stopped counting after discovering a window in the reflection of his own office door.

## The Log Written by Salt

The chart unfolded into a book when placed upon wet wood. Its pages contained no dates, only descriptions of rooms abandoned by their sleepers.

One room belonged to a woman who had died before the harbor existed. Another belonged to a child still unborn. A third described the harbor master's office, including the coat upon its hook and the cloth lying beneath it.

He turned the page before finishing that entry. On the next page, the office was empty. Its window faced a pale deck, and the sea beyond it contained a town with no harbor.

The book was closed and bound with rope. Salt gathered upon the knot in the shape of a small ship. At night, the rope could be heard tightening around something that breathed slowly within the pages.

## The Serpent beneath the Keel

On certain nights, a long pale creature is visible below the vessel. Its scales resemble the brass keys found in the returning boats. Its fins unfold like wings, moving through water that remains perfectly still.

The creature's head never faces the harbor. It turns instead toward a place below its own body, as though guarding a second ship hidden inside the first ship's shadow.

A fisherman lowered a lantern to observe it. The lantern's reflection descended while the lantern remained in his hand. Far below, the reflection reached a small window. Someone behind that window extinguished it.

The fisherman's lantern continued burning. From that night onward, its flame cast the shadow of an anchor upon every wall except those facing the sea.

## The Words inside the Sleeves

**Oryl — khae — vex — thuun.**

These sounds were discovered woven into the coat's lining. They could be read only when both sleeves were filled with seawater. The water did not spill, even when the coat was held upright.

The harbor master attempted to pronounce the sequence. Each sound caused a brass key to turn beneath his desk. By the fourth, a narrow seam appeared in the floor, revealing a pale deck under an unfamiliar sky.

He emptied the sleeves into a bucket. The seam closed. The bucket reflected the illuminated window, though there was no reflection of the man who held it.

The coat has remained dry ever since. Its lining occasionally makes the sound of waves reaching a shore too far away to appear upon the chart.

## The Passenger Who Was Not Listed

> Who keeps the cabins empty?
>
> The sleeper whose room is still ashore.
>
> Why does the anchor hang above the sea?
>
> Because something beneath it has already arrived.
>
> Which key opens the captain's door?
>
> The one that becomes warm before the window goes dark.

These exchanges were recorded by a fisherman listening to the coat through a shell. The speaker paused after every answer, leaving enough silence for a door to close.

The final pause did not end. The fisherman placed the shell beside the bound book. Its opening remains turned toward the rope, waiting for the next sound.

## A Harbor without a Tide

The vessel continues appearing beyond the harbor. The townspeople keep their keys in bowls lined with dry sand. Nobody has found a warm one, though several have begun to cast shadows resembling doorways.

The harbor master no longer wears a coat when visiting the shore. He watches the anchor and listens for the tightening rope in the office behind him.

Orylkhaevex carries the rooms that have not finished departing. Beneath its pale deck, a serpent follows the shadow of a ship whose passengers remain asleep somewhere else.
